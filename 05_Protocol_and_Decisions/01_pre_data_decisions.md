# Pre-data design decisions

## Typed measurement model

The study distinguishes world-state continuity, presented evidence, observer estimates, observed judgments, and normative truth. The empirical path is:

\[
E_k \longrightarrow \hat C_k^{(O)} \longrightarrow J_q^{(O)}.
\]

The vignettes manipulate evidence, not ontic continuity directly and not the participant's internal estimate directly.

## Five evidence dimensions

The experimental design used body, memory, function, self-identification, and transition evidence. Self-identification was named `E_selfID` rather than an ontic `C_self` because participants observe a report. Transition was denoted `Pi_transition` to avoid claiming in advance that the wording isolated pure causal continuity.

## Scope of the observer

Participants judged as third-party observers. Prior observer-target relationship/history was therefore held fixed and absent by design. This study estimates a controlled observer slice rather than the full many-body problem.

## Excluded sixth axis

Relationship continuity was not treated as a sixth manipulated continuity cue. It was retained as a downstream query because treating it as both input and outcome would mix types.

## Ten-condition hybrid pilot

The pilot combined single-cue perturbations with two local transition stress tests. The shared cells allow exploratory Body × Transition and Memory × Transition contrasts without running a full factorial design.

## Separate arms

Manipulation checks and judgment outcomes were assigned to separate arms to limit demand characteristics and participant burden. Every participant still viewed all 10 conditions in randomized order.

## Extreme anchors

- Deny was retained as a self-identification conflict anchor, not automatically treated as the ordinary low level of a factorial factor.
- Parallel was retained as a coexistence/duplication anchor, not treated as the third level of transition continuity.
- Neither anchor appeared first; both were restricted to positions 5–10.

## Deny hypotheses

Before the completed dataset was inspected, Deny was expected to distinguish an epistemic veto from a normative veto. The possibility that Deny would approximate a strong or complete veto (“Case B”) was explicitly considered before data collection.

## Parallel outcomes

Parallel included pairwise same-person judgments and an exclusivity item so that non-exclusive or transitivity-like patterns could be observed rather than ruled out by the questionnaire.
